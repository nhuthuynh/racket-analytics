## 3. 5 Whys on the largest miss: 0 of 32 units meet the DoD

1. **Why is no unit DoD-done?** The story DoD needs a green `ci-gate` on GitHub for the story's commits and no open Blocking review finding. Neither is true.
2. **Why is there no green CI run?** CI ran once on `sprint-01` (`2390e9a`, failure). It never ran on the round-1, round-2 or round-3 fixes.
3. **Why did it not run again?** `ci.yml` runs only on PRs, pushes to `main`, schedule and manual dispatch. Agents may not push or open PRs, so each run needs the human to push and dispatch. That happened once, and every round re-raised it (QA-R1-06, PE-R2-02, QA-R2-02, PE-R3-05, QA-R3-05).
4. **Why did the escalation not change the mechanism?** The EM escalated each **instance** ("please dispatch"), not the **mechanism** (who may push, and which branches trigger CI). The PO was never asked for a standing decision (judgment).
5. **Why?** The Sprint 0 action A1 framed CI as a one-off repo-admin setup. Its success measure (3 nightlies) has no owner for each round's run. **Root cause: the delivery workflow has no automatic route from a fix round to CI. CI evidence depends on an unscheduled human step.** → Decision D1 for the PO (sprint-report §6), action A1.

The second-largest miss is the open blockers, analysed as M1 below.

The scoring engine went from `46 failed, 8 errors` to green with 99% coverage. ## 6. Focus-area findings

- **Estimation (ADR 0010).** The build cost was again small compared with the units: the build fit in one session. The units that slipped were all external inputs or a test dispute. The real hidden cost is the review loop: 3 rounds, 18 fix commits after the smoke (22% of commits), and about 25 open findings carried over. Recommendation for Sprint 2 planning (judgment): keep load factor 0.8; reserve about 10 units for the Sprint 1 carry-over (sprint-report §7); and plan the review loop explicitly as about 20% of each lane's capacity.
- **QA writes tests first.** The split worked for domain and API behaviour: no seam conflicts, and BE and FE built against executable specs. It worked less well for browser helpers and environment-dependent tests. There was a sign-in race in a QA helper (QA-R1-01), whole-bucket diffs (QA-R3-02), and a test rounding change that hid real behaviour (M7). Contract-driven test changes produced about 20 test-change rows; that is the cost of tests landing before the contract was final.
