## 3. 5 Whys on the largest miss: the goal is not demonstrated, and G01-11 has never been 0

1. **Why is the goal not met?** G01-11 has been 10-17 open blocker/major findings in every round, and G01-05 ran below the disk floor.
2. **Why do findings stay open?** 11 of the 17 need a human: P1 (CI, nightly, WebKit), P2 (ASVS 6.3.3), P3 (phone clips), P4 (carry-over approval), or the P7 design review. The other 6 are new defects from round 3 that never got a row, so nobody worked on them (M10).
3. **Why did the human items stay open for a whole sprint?** Each was raised again in each round as a finding, not as one decision request with a deadline. The single PO list (P1-P4) only appeared in sprint-close review round 2. P1 was finally actioned at `7a9e41e`, 6 rounds after it was first raised.
4. **Why were new defects invisible?** The goal rounds counted rows in `review-rounds.md`, and only owners wrote rows. Findings that no owner picked up existed only in the orchestrator's output.
5. **Root causes:** (a) **the tracking default was "not recorded", not "open"**: a finding entered the record only when someone acted on it (fixed by ADR 0033 rule 1); (b) **decisions that need the PO were raised per finding, not batched with a date** (fixed in practice by the P-list; action A5 makes it the standard).

## 6. Focus-area findings

- **Closing a sprint under the PO standing rule** cost more than building it. The build fit in one session. The close took 6 rounds and 49 commits (35% of the sprint), and still ended "no". Two things decide the close: the goal **metrics** (10 of 12 met, mostly early) and the **open findings** (never 0). Recommendation for Sprint 2 (judgment): write the scorecard at planning (as here), dry-run it at mid-sprint (ADR 0033 rule 2), and send the PO list (P-items with dates) on day 1 of the close, not after round 2.
- **Estimation (ADR 0010, kept from the first draft).** The implemented ratio is 0.906. All 3 missing units are external. Keep load factor 0.8. Reserve about 12 units for the Sprint 1 carry-over (sprint-report §8), and plan the review and verification loop as about 20% of each lane's capacity.
- **QA writes tests first (kept).** It worked for domain and API behaviour. It worked less well for timing-sensitive browser tests (QA-R3-E2E-01) and for boundary fuzzing (M15).
